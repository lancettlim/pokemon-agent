Usage

How often each Pokémon is brought, and how its teams do, in Champions tournaments.

How this is computed

Every figure is computed from the team sheets of Champions-format tournaments only, never blended with standard VGC events. Usage is the share of teams that brought a Pokémon; hover a figure for its count and its share of roster slots (a team has six). The Ladder view is the exception: Showdown ladder statistics, shown beside tournament usage and never added to it.

Type

Ranked by the 95% Wilson lower bound, so a small sample can't top the list on luck.

Popular is not the same as successful: usage weighted by where each team finished.

How this is computed

Raw usage counts a last-place team's roster slot exactly like a winner's. Top cut counts only appearances on a team that placed 1–8; placement-weighted weights every appearance by 1/placement, so a win counts far more than a 200th with no cutoff cliff.

The Movement column is how many places a Pokémon gains or loses versus its raw usage rank. A positive number is a quiet top-cut staple; a negative one is a crowd favourite that underperforms.

What the Showdown ladder plays more, or less, than tournament players do.

How this is computed

Ladder usage is Smogon's monthly statistics for Pokémon Showdown's Champions ladder: each Pokémon's share of teams across every rated battle in the regulation's month. At 1760+ each battle is weighted by how likely its players are rated above 1760, so the strongest players count most; All ratings counts every battle the same. The month is the newest one the regulation was in force for at least half of.

Tournament usage is the share of the same regulation's official tournament teams that brought it. Both are shares of teams, so they sit on one scale, but they come from different players and are never added together. Credit: Smogon University and Pokémon Showdown.

Pokémon Profile

Which Pokémon most often share a team with this one.

How this is computed

Co-occurrence is the raw pairing rate, which mostly re-ranks whoever is individually popular. Synergy ranks by lift: how much more often the pair appears together than their individual popularity alone predicts (×1.0 is exactly chance). Synergy only counts pairs seen on at least 5 real teams, since lift is noisy below that.

The top-placing teams in this regulation that brought this Pokémon.

How hard the regulation's attacks hit this Pokémon, by type.

How this is computed

Each attacking type is weighted by how often the regulation's teams use damaging moves of that type: a Pokémon's usage share times each damaging move's share of its moveset. The multiplier is the standard type chart against this Pokémon's types. This is type-based, not battle results: it ignores abilities, items, Tera-less STAB and who actually brings what into which game.

Showdown ladder, from battle logs

The opponents that most often KO this Pokémon or force it out.

How this is computed

Smogon reads every rated ladder battle in the regulation's month. Each time this Pokémon and an opponent were both in and one of them was KO'd or switched out, that counts as an encounter; the rate is how often it was this Pokémon. Opponents are ranked as Smogon ranks them, by the rate less four standard deviations, so a rate seen in few encounters ranks lower. Only opponents met at least 100 times are listed.

This is the only Pokémon-vs-Pokémon signal on the site, and it is ladder data from all ratings: Smogon lists a pair only above about 20 encounters, and weighted toward 1760+ players almost none clear that. Tournament results are team vs team.

    Detected Archetypes

    Experimental team identities, each anchored by a three-Pokémon core that recurs across events.

    How this is computed

    Qualifying cores appear on at least five teams and have above-chance three-way and average pair lift. Cores sharing two members consolidate under their strongest neighbour; each team gets one primary assignment and may keep one near-tied secondary one.

    The names are neutral on purpose: composition alone does not prove an editorial label such as “Rain” or “Balance.”

    Matchup

    A damage estimate, type effectiveness and real head-to-head records for two Pokémon.

    How this is computed

    The calculator assumes level 50 with full investment, 32 Stat Points, in the attacking and defending stat (tournament team sheets publish no Stat Points). It models STAB, type effectiveness, stat stages, weather and the listed items and abilities. It does not model burns, critical hits, multi-hit moves or other items and abilities.

    Head-to-head is measured team vs team: no source publishes which Pokémon were brought to a given match.

    Head-to-head record

    How often teams fielding the attacker actually beat teams fielding the defender, from real tournament pairings. Ranked by a 95% Wilson lower bound, so a 1‑0 record doesn't outrank a well-established one. This is a team-level result, not a claim that these two Pokémon fought each other.

    Matchup profile

    Each side's overall record across every decided match its teams played, and its single best and worst opponent — both chosen by Wilson lower bound over a minimum sample, so a 1‑0 record never reads as a best matchup.

    Pokémon most often teamed with the defender in real tournament rosters. This is a teammate-pairing view, not a matchup signal — the head-to-head section above is the matchup signal.

          Data & Sources

          Where every number on this site comes from, and whether it passed the dataset's release gates.

          About provenance

          Every record in the dataset carries its source, source URL, upstream record id and extraction time. Records without a traceable source never ship.

          What the site's numbers mean, and how much to trust each one.

          • Everything is scoped to one regulation, the one in the header. Switch it there; links keep it as ?reg=.
          • Every percentage has a denominator. Hover a figure (or see its row) for "N of M"; sample sizes appear as n.
          • Small samples say so. Win rates rank by their Wilson bound, deltas are withheld below 100 teams, and thin sets are marked.
          • Matchups are team vs team. No source says which four Pokémon were brought to a game.
          • There are no Stat Point spreads in tournament data. Team sheets publish ability, item, Stat Alignment (nature) and moves only; anything that needs stats assumes full investment: 32 Stat Points and IVs fixed at 31.

          Standing limitations that apply to figures elsewhere on this dashboard. Each is a property of what the sources publish, not a bug to be fixed here.

          • Head-to-head is team vs team, never Pokémon vs Pokémon. No source publishes a per-battle log naming which four of six were actually brought, so a "matchup" figure means teams containing A beat teams containing B — it is attributed to the whole roster. The one Pokémon-vs-Pokémon figure on the site is a profile's Checks and counters, which comes from Showdown ladder battle logs, not tournaments.
          • Matchup appearance counts overstate sample roughly 6× for the same reason: every pairing is counted against all six Pokémon on each side.
          • The legal pool is an upper bound. PokéBase publishes no removal signal, so the cumulative legal-Pokémon count can only grow — a later-banned Pokémon is never subtracted.
          • Signature share is structurally capped at 16.7% — a Pokémon can appear only once on a six-slot team — which is why players are ranked by share of their teams instead.
          • Everything here is Champions-format only. The tournament corpus also covers standard VGC events; every usage and win-rate figure is filtered to Champions events first. Blending the two would be comparing different games.
          • Tournament data has no Stat Point spreads. Official team sheets publish ability, held item, Stat Alignment (nature) and moves and nothing more. The damage calculator therefore assumes a fully invested level-50 baseline on both sides (32 Stat Points, IVs fixed at 31) rather than any real spread. A profile's Spreads panel shows what Showdown ladder players ran, a different population; no tournament view uses it.
          • The Showdown ladder is its own population. Smogon's monthly statistics count every rated ladder battle, weighted toward 1760+ players. They sit beside tournament figures in the Usage tab's Ladder view and the profile's Counters and Spreads, are labelled there, and are never added to a tournament figure.
          • Legality has a single snapshot date, so nothing on this dashboard trends legality over time; usage trends are measured across tournament dates instead.
          • Champions stat deltas are all zero. No rebalance has been published yet, so every Pokémon's Champions stats still equal its canonical stats.
